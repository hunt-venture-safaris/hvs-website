> **In short:** Trophy hunting permits in Pakistan are issued within national quotas and allocated to licensed Pakistani outfitters through an open government auction. Hunt Venture Safaris secures the permit, pays the provincial shooting licence fee and handles the permit formalities as part of your package. Your trophy also needs CITES paperwork: Pakistan issues the export side, while any import permit for your home country is your responsibility, and requirements differ from country to country.

## How permits are allocated (open government auction)

Each season, Pakistan's Ministry of Climate Change and Environmental Coordination, acting through the CITES Management Authority of Pakistan, approves trophy hunting quotas for markhor, Himalayan ibex, Sindh ibex, Blanford urial, Punjab urial, blue sheep and other species, based on population data from the provincial wildlife departments ([Ministry of Climate Change, December 2025](https://mocc.gov.pk/NewsDetail/OTdiNmY5NzctMjNkOC00M2QxLThhNDctMTgxMzFhNWRkNmZj)).

Within those quotas, permits are allocated to Pakistani outfitters through an open government auction. In Gilgit-Baltistan the auction is held each autumn by the Gilgit-Baltistan Wildlife and Parks Department in Gilgit. For the 2026–27 season it offered 117 permits: 3 Astore Markhor, 14 Blue Sheep and 100 Himalayan Ibex ([Dawn, September 2026](https://www.dawn.com/news/2028988)). Numbers are set again every season, so the markhor permits in particular are few and are committed early.

How permits for the southern hunts (Sindh–Balochistan and Punjab) are allocated by the relevant provincial departments is something we will explain for your specific hunt.

## What the auction means for your rate

Our 2027–28 rates are based on the trophy permit fees for that season. Because the fees are set at auction, they can change from one season to the next. Our policy is straightforward:

- Permits are allocated to Pakistani outfitters through an open government auction.
- Minor increases are absorbed by Hunt Venture Safaris.
- A substantial rise in permit fees will be communicated and invoiced separately.

We publish no prices on this site. Write to us for a quotation for one or two hunters, a non-hunting companion or extra hunting days. Payment is 40% in advance, with the balance due before the hunt starts.

Every Hunt Venture Safaris package includes:

## Community-controlled hunting areas

Our Gilgit-Baltistan hunts take place in community-controlled hunting areas, where local communities manage the land and share in the permit revenue. For the 2025–26 season, Dawn reported that local communities receive about 80% of hunting revenues for conservation and development projects ([Dawn, September 2025](https://www.dawn.com/news/1939432)). Shares are set by the authorities and may change; we do not set them. More on this is on our [conservation page](/conservation/).

## CITES and import permits for your home country

CITES, the international convention on trade in endangered species, governs the movement of most of the species we hunt. At the time of writing:

| Species | CITES listing |
|---|---|
| Astore Markhor (*Capra falconeri falconeri*) | Appendix I |
| Himalayan Ibex | Appendix III (listed by Pakistan) |
| Blue Sheep (*Pseudois nayaur*) | Appendix III (listed by Pakistan) |
| Sindh Ibex | Appendix III (listed by Pakistan) |
| Blanford Urial, Punjab Urial | Listed; the appendix depends on the subspecies. We confirm it for your hunt |

CITES listings are revised from time to time. Check the current listing for your species on [Species+](https://speciesplus.net), the official CITES database, and we will confirm the documents your country requires.

Markhor are a special case. Under CITES Resolution Conf. 10.15 (Rev. CoP14), Pakistan has an annual export quota for markhor hunting trophies, each trophy must carry a self-locking tag recording the exporting country, its number within the quota and the year, and the importing country should treat it as a personal item, generally limited to one markhor trophy per hunter per calendar year ([CITES Resolution Conf. 10.15](https://cites.org/sites/default/files/documents/COP/19/resolution/F-Res-10-15-R14.pdf)). As an Appendix I species, a markhor trophy normally needs an import permit from your own country as well as Pakistan's export permit.

What your country asks for varies:

- **United States:** Appendix I trophies need a US import permit from the US Fish and Wildlife Service before import, and every trophy is declared to FWS at a designated port ([FWS guidance](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/OLE%20Personal%20Wildlife%20Shipments%20-%20UPDATED%20Importing%20Personal%20Sport%20Hunted%20Trophies.pdf)). Some markhor are also listed under the US Endangered Species Act; confirm with FWS whether an additional permit applies to the Astore Markhor.
- **European Union:** markhor falls under Annex A of the EU wildlife trade rules, and Annex A hunting trophies need an import permit from your member state's CITES authority. Rules for the other species differ.
- **Elsewhere:** check with your national CITES authority.

Rules change, so confirm the current requirements with your own authority and with us before you book. Requirements vary by country; we will confirm the documents for yours.
