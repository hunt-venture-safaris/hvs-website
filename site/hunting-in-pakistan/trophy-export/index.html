Hunting in Pakistan

Exporting your trophy from Pakistan

Your trophy is prepared and packed in the field as part of every hunt. Shipping it home, and clearing it into your own country, is a separate step with its own paperwork and costs.

Updated 8 November 2026

In short: Hunt Venture Safaris includes trophy skinning, salting and packing, and the trophy permit formalities and fees. Trophy export from Pakistan, shipment, customs clearance and excess baggage are not included and are paid separately. Most of our species are CITES-listed, so the trophy travels with an export document from Pakistan, and your own country may require an import permit before it arrives.

What is included

Every package covers the work done in Pakistan to prepare your trophy:

  • Trophy skinning, salting and packing, carried out by our field staff after the hunt.
  • Trophy permit formalities and fees, handled by us with the wildlife authorities.
  • Government liaison for permits and documents, so the paperwork that follows the trophy starts from a correct permit.

Field preparation matters: a well-skinned, properly salted cape and clean horns travel better and are easier for your taxidermist.

What is not included

The following are excluded from all packages and are arranged and paid separately:

  • trophy export from Pakistan
  • shipment of the trophy
  • customs clearance (in Pakistan and at home)
  • excess baggage
  • any service not listed as included

Your home-country import permit, inspection and broker fees, and taxidermy are also your responsibility.

How export is arranged

The trophy leaves Pakistan with a CITES export permit where the species is CITES-listed. Astore Markhor are CITES Appendix I; Himalayan Ibex, Blue Sheep and Sindh Ibex are listed in Appendix III by Pakistan; urials are also listed (see Permits and CITES for the table).

For markhor, Pakistan's exports are governed by a specific CITES resolution: each trophy carries a self-locking tag showing the exporting country, its number within Pakistan's annual quota and the year, and the same details appear on the export document (CITES Resolution Conf. 10.15). Because markhor is Appendix I, the export permit generally cannot be issued until your country's import permit has been granted (or your authority has given written assurance that it will be). That is why the import application should start early.

How the shipment itself is organised:

  • Shipping agent: we advise on how and from where your trophy is shipped, and put you in touch with the agent who handles it.
  • Hand baggage or cargo: ask us which option applies to your species and your home country.
  • Costs: quoted separately by the agent according to weight, destination and documents. We do not publish them.

Timelines

The time from the end of your hunt to the trophy arriving home depends on three things: the Pakistani export paperwork, your home-country import permit, and the shipping route.

What speeds it up:

  • applying for any home-country import permit as soon as your hunt is confirmed
  • sending us copies of permits and assurances as soon as you receive them
  • naming your taxidermist or receiving agent before the hunt

Your home-country import

Import rules are set by your own country, change from time to time, and differ by species. Some examples, which you should confirm with your authority before booking:

  • United States. Every wildlife import is declared to the US Fish and Wildlife Service on Form 3-177 and normally enters through a designated port. Appendix I trophies such as markhor need a US import permit obtained before import, alongside a valid export document from Pakistan (FWS guidance). Some markhor are also listed under the Endangered Species Act; confirm with FWS whether an additional permit applies.
  • European Union. Markhor is in Annex A of the EU wildlife trade rules; Annex A hunting trophies need an import permit from your member state's CITES authority, and the personal-effects exemption does not apply to them (CITES summary of EU rules). Requirements for the other species differ.
  • Other countries. Contact your national CITES Management Authority.

Many hunters use a specialist trophy import broker or taxidermist at home to handle this side. We will send you whatever documents from Pakistan they need.
