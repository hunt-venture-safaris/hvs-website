## 1. Brand essence

### What HVS is

Hunt Venture Safaris (Pvt) Limited is a government-licensed trophy hunting outfitter (Govt. Licence No. 2087) based in Gilgit, Gilgit-Baltistan, operating since 1989. It organises mountain hunts for Astore Markhor, Himalayan Ibex and Blue Sheep in Gilgit-Baltistan, and for Blanford Urial, Sindh Ibex and Punjab Urial in southern Pakistan, alongside tours, treks, jeep safaris and mountaineering expeditions across the Karakoram, Himalaya and Hindu Kush.

### Positioning statement

> For experienced international hunters and the agents who book them, Hunt Venture Safaris is the Gilgit-based, government-licensed outfitter that has run legal mountain hunts in Pakistan since 1989 — handling the permit, the paperwork and the mountain, in community-controlled areas where every hunt pays back into conservation and local people.

### Brand promise

**A legal, well-run hunt in the high mountains of Pakistan, with nothing left to chance on paper.** The hunter's job is the mountain; ours is everything else — permit, licence, firearm documents, flights to Gilgit, camp, guides, and the trophy packed for the journey home.

### Brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means | Evidence from the brochures |
|---|---|---|
| **Licensed and lawful** | Every hunt sits inside Pakistan's permit system, and we say so plainly. | "Government Licence No. 2087"; inclusions list "Trophy hunting permit", "Provincial shooting licence fee", "Firearm import/export documentation", "Government liaison for permits and documents"; permits "allocated to Pakistani outfitters through an open government auction". |
| **Rooted since 1989** | A Gilgit company, not an agent reselling someone else's hunt. Decades of local relationships. | "A Legacy Since 1989"; "One of the pioneering and most trusted trophy hunting companies in Gilgit-Baltistan"; office in Chinar Bagh, Gilgit; vintage film photographs from early hunts. |
| **Community and conservation** | The hunt happens on community land and funds both the species and the people who protect it. | "Travel to the community-controlled hunting area" (every itinerary); "Every hunt also contributes to the conservation of these species and the development of local communities"; team of "local guides and conservationists". |
| **The whole expedition, handled** | Full-board, Islamabad-to-Islamabad logistics with honest terms. | "Islamabad to Islamabad"; "Full board"; "4x4 transport, incl. if flights are cancelled"; "Trophy skinning, salting and packing"; clear "Not included" list; "Minor increases are absorbed by Hunt Venture Safaris". |

Use the pillars to decide what to say first. If a sentence supports none of them, cut it.

## 2. Logo

### Anatomy

The master logo is a horizontal lockup of three parts.

| Element | Description | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Monogram "HVS"** | Large, light, high-contrast serif capitals in graphite grey. Thin hairlines, heavy stems. | Establishment, permanence, a company with a name to protect. The restraint of grey rather than black reads as quiet confidence. |
| **Rearing markhor** | Silhouette in graphite and black rising out of the "V", forelegs raised, spiral horns sweeping back. | The flagship species (Pakistan's national animal) and the mountain. Rising from the "V" ties the company to the animal: the hunt is the company. |
| **Crimson horn tips** | The only colour in the mark, on the horn tips. | Scarcity and value: the trophy, the summit, the moment. This is why crimson is rationed across the brand (see section 3). |
| **Wordmark** | "HUNT VENTURE SAFARIS / (PVT) LIMITED" in bold black serif capitals, two lines, set to the right of the monogram and aligned to its baseline. | The legal entity. "(Pvt) Limited" matters to agents and authorities — it is a registered company. |

### Lockups to define

| Lockup | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Full horizontal lockup** | `hvs-logo.png`, as supplied | Default. Website header on wide screens if space allows, brochure covers, letterhead, email signature, stand fascia. |
| **B. Compact mark** | `hvs-mark.png` — "HVS" + markhor, cropped from the master | Favicon, app/touch icons, social avatars, WhatsApp Business profile, mobile navigation, small footers, watermark on photographs. |
| **C. Stacked lockup** (as on the species brochure covers) | Compact mark above a typeset "HUNT VENTURE SAFARIS / (PVT) LIMITED" and "Govt. Licence No. 2087" | Portrait formats: brochure covers, roll-up banners, business-card front. Build it only from the vector (see below); do not re-typeset the wordmark in a substitute font. |

The compact mark **must be cropped from the master, never redrawn or traced by hand.** The file supplied here was produced by cropping the master at the gap between the "S" and the wordmark and trimming to content.

### Clear space

Unit of measure: **H** = the cap height of the "H" in the monogram (126 px on the 324 px master, about 39% of the lockup's height).

- Full lockup: keep at least **½ H** clear on all sides. Nothing — text, image edges, rules, other logos — enters that zone.
- Compact mark: keep at least **¼ of the mark's width** clear on all sides; inside a circular avatar crop, the mark's widest point must sit inside 80% of the circle's diameter.
- On a plate (see below), the plate's padding is the clear space: at least ½ H.

### Minimum sizes

| Lockup | Web (CSS px) | Print |
|---|---|---|
| Full lockup | 200 px wide (240 px preferred). Below this the wordmark capitals fall under 8 px. | 50 mm wide |
| Stacked lockup | 120 px wide | 30 mm wide |
| Compact mark | 32 px tall. At favicon 16 px, test legibility; if the markhor turns to mush, a simplified favicon must come from the vector redraw, not from editing the PNG. | 12 mm tall |

Always supply @2x and @3x raster exports for screens. Until a vector exists, never scale the PNG master above its native size (985 px wide); large-format print must wait for the SVG.

### Backgrounds

The master is **dark-on-light only**: graphite and black on transparent. On the brochures' dark field it disappears (the wordmark becomes near-invisible).

1. **On light grounds** (white, bone, cream, pale photography): place the master directly.
2. **On dark grounds or photographs — now:** place the master on a **bone plate**, exactly as the brochure covers do: a solid off-white rectangle (sampled from the covers at roughly #E7E4DC; exact value in the design system), small corner radius, padding of at least ½ H, placed top-left on covers. No drop shadow, no transparency in the plate.
3. **On dark grounds — after the redraw:** use a **reversed version**. A designer must produce:
   - **Reversed lockup:** monogram, markhor and wordmark in single-colour white (or bone), **crimson horn tips retained**. Internal black/graphite detail in the markhor that separates head, horns and legs must be redrawn as negative space so it still reads in one colour.
   - **Single-colour versions:** all-white, all-black and all-graphite, with no crimson, for embossing, engraving, fax, single-colour print and watermarks.
   - All of these must be made from the vector redraw. **Do not invert or recolour the PNG** — the anti-aliasing and the two-tone markhor will break.

### Recommendation: commission a vectorisation

Commission a clean SVG/PDF vectorisation of the existing mark from a professional illustrator. This is a **redraw, not a redesign**: same letterforms, same markhor pose, same proportions, same colours. Deliverables: master SVG and EPS/PDF for lockups A, B and C; full-colour, reversed, and single-colour versions; a 16 px simplified favicon; the wordmark typeface identified (or redrawn as outlines) so it can be reproduced. Budget for one round of client approval against the PNG.

### Misuse

Do not:

- Place the master directly on the dark field, a dark panel or a busy photograph without a plate.
- Invert, recolour, add gradients to or "brighten" the PNG to make it work on dark.
- Move the crimson anywhere else in the mark, add more crimson, or make the horn tips another colour.
- Redraw, trace or "tidy" the markhor or the monogram in-house.
- Re-typeset "HUNT VENTURE SAFARIS (PVT) LIMITED" in another font beside the monogram.
- Stretch, skew, rotate, outline, emboss, bevel or add a drop shadow or glow.
- Separate the markhor from the "HVS" or use the markhor alone as a logo.
- Rearrange the lockup (wordmark above or left of the monogram).
- Crop into the horns or the "S" on social avatars.
- Place the logo inside an animal photograph so it touches the animal or the hunter.

## 3. Colour story

The palette comes from two places: the logo (crimson, graphite, black) and the brochures (field, panel, brass, white). Exact values, tints and contrast-checked pairings are defined in the design system. This section defines **roles**.

| Role | Source | Story | Use for | Do not use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Field** (very dark green-black) | Brochure background | The night mountain: the colour of a valley before dawn when the glassing starts. | Primary dark ground for site sections, brochure pages, stand backdrop. | Body text on light grounds (use black/graphite instead). |
| **Panel** (raised dark green) | Brochure cards | The same mountain, one step nearer. | Cards, at-a-glance blocks, itinerary rows, footer bands on field. | Large flat areas on their own — panel only means something on field. |
| **Brass** | Brochure rules and eyebrows | Craft, trophy, the brass of a rifle case or a measuring tape. | Hairline rules, small eyebrow labels, data labels ("SPECIES", "ALTITUDE"), itinerary markers, fine dividers. | Body text, buttons, large fills, text on bone (contrast fails). |
| **Crimson** | Logo horn tips; brochure "active day" marker and "Not included" header | The trophy moment. As scarce in use as on the mark. | Primary action (one per screen), the active/hunting-days marker, a single critical status (e.g. "Permit fees may rise"). | Decoration, headings, backgrounds, icons in bulk, anything that suggests blood. Crimson text on field fails contrast — use it as a fill with white text, or on light grounds. |
| **Graphite** | Logo monogram | Stone, the company's own voice. | Secondary text and icons on light grounds; the monogram. | Text on field (contrast fails). |
| **Black** | Logo wordmark | The legal name. | Primary text on bone/white; wordmark. | — |
| **Bone / cream** | Brochure logo plate | Snow in shadow, paper. | Logo plate, light sections on the site, business cards, print stock. | Large site-wide backgrounds paired with a serif and a warm accent — that is the generic "cream and terracotta" look the client asked us to avoid. Keep bone as a plate and a secondary ground, not the brand's main surface. |
| **White** | Brochure text | — | Text on field and panel. | — |

### Proportions

Approximate share of any composed surface (page, slide, banner):

| Dark mode (default, as brochures) | Share |
|---|---|
| Field + panel | 60–70% (photography takes most of the rest) |
| White text | 10–15% |
| Brass | 2–5% (lines and labels only) |
| Crimson | ≤1% — one action or one marker |
| Bone | Logo plate only |

On light sections, bone/white takes the field's place, black and graphite carry text, brass becomes a rule colour only, and crimson stays at one action.

**Rule of one:** on any single screen, page or panel, crimson appears **once**. If you need a second emphasis, use brass or weight, not more crimson.

## 4. Typography character

Exact families, weights and the type scale are decided in the design system. This section sets direction.

**What we have.** The logo uses a light, high-contrast serif for "HVS" and a bold serif for the wordmark. The brochures use Arimo (an Arial-metric sans) for headings and body, with Argent CF (a high-contrast serif) for the cover tagline. The brochures read cleanly but the sans headings say little about the brand.

**Direction.** Pair a **refined high-contrast serif for display** — sparing, large, used for page titles, hunt names and pull quotes, echoing the hairlines of the monogram — with a **clean humanist or grotesque sans for everything else**: body, data, labels, navigation, forms. The serif carries heritage; the sans carries facts. Do not set body copy, tables or itineraries in the serif.

| Role | Candidate (Google Fonts) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Display serif | **Cormorant Garamond** | Light weights with very fine hairlines, closest in spirit to the monogram's thin-and-thick strokes; elegant at large sizes; full Latin Extended for Spanish. Weak at small sizes, so display only. |
| Display serif | **Bodoni Moda** | Didone contrast with optical sizing, so it stays crisp from 28 px to poster scale; a sharper, more formal echo of the "HVS" capitals. |
| Text sans | **Source Sans 3** | Humanist, highly legible at small sizes and for older readers, generous figures for itineraries and altitudes, strong Spanish/European diacritic support, variable weights. |
| Text sans | **Public Sans** | Neutral grotesque built for public-information legibility; plain like the brochures' Arimo but better finished, so the site and the brochures still feel related. |

Avoid Playfair Display (over-used in templated sites). If the client wants continuity with the printed brochures, Arimo remains acceptable for print until the next reprint. Argent CF is a commercial font; using it on the web needs a licence — TBC (client to confirm whether they hold one).

**Typographic habits to keep from the brochures:**

- **Eyebrow labels**: small, uppercase, letter-spaced, brass ("WHO WE ARE", "AT A GLANCE", "PROPOSED HUNTING PLAN").
- **Headings in title case, plain weight**: "A Legacy Since 1989", "20-Day Itinerary".
- **Middle dot separators** in metadata lines: "20 days · 15 hunting days".
- **En dash with spaces for ranges in running text**: "2,500 – 4,700 m"; tight en dash in compact data: "2027–28".

## 5. Voice and tone

### Who we speak to

- **Experienced international hunters** — US/SCI members, Spanish and wider European hunters (Cinegética), Gulf and Russia/CIS clients. Often older, well travelled, have hunted on several continents. They know what a markhor is. They want facts, legality and trust, not adjectives.
- **Booking agents and hunting consultants** — they resell our hunts. They need exact terms, inclusions, permit mechanics, dates and quick answers they can forward.
- **Non-hunting companions** — want to know about comfort, safety and what they will do.

### Tone principles

1. **Factual first.** Lead with the number, the place, the permit. Let the mountain do the impressing.
2. **Quietly confident.** We have been doing this since 1989; we do not need superlatives.
3. **Respectful of the animal.** Name it properly, describe it accurately, never mock or gloat.
4. **Plain about money and law.** Say what is included, what is not, and what could change.
5. **Warm, not chatty.** A host's courtesy, not a marketer's enthusiasm.

### Rewrites from the brochures

| Brochure says | Problem | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "offering a unique opportunity to experience the rich and diverse wildlife of Pakistan" | Generic; says nothing a competitor could not. | "We have guided hunters in Gilgit-Baltistan since 1989, in the community-controlled areas of Astore, Hunza, Nagar and Skardu." |
| "creating unforgettable hunting adventures in some of the world's most extraordinary landscapes" | Two superlatives, no fact. | "Our hunts run between 2,500 and 4,700 metres in the Karakoram, Himalaya and Hindu Kush." |
| "One of the pioneering and most trusted trophy hunting companies" | Unprovable "most trusted". | "Licensed by the Government of Pakistan (No. 2087) and hunting in Gilgit-Baltistan since 1989." Keep "pioneering" only if the client can support it (see open questions). |
| "the blue sheep offers an exhilarating hunt in breathtaking landscapes" | Adjectives in place of information. | "Blue Sheep are hunted in the Shimshal Valley at 3,200–4,500 m, reached by a two-day trek." |
| "Known for its majestic spiral horns, the Astore markhor is a highly sought-after trophy" | Acceptable, but "highly sought-after" is filler. | "The Astore Markhor carries the flared, spiralling horns that make it Pakistan's national animal and one of the most demanding mountain hunts in Asia." (Confirm "national animal" wording with client — factually the markhor is Pakistan's national animal.) |
| "Every hunt also contributes to the conservation of these species and the development of local communities" | True in spirit but unspecific. | "Hunts take place in community-controlled areas. Under Pakistan's trophy hunting programme, a share of each permit fee goes to the local community." **Only publish the share or amounts once the client confirms them.** |

| "Fifteen full days for the hunting expedition" | Fine — keep. Factual and calm. | — |

### The animal and the kill

- Describe the animal with respect and accuracy: species, horn form, habitat, behaviour. "A mature Astore Markhor", not "a monster billy".
- Use **"take"**, **"harvest"** or **"hunt"**; avoid "kill", "shoot down", "bag", "slay", "drop".
- No gore, no wound description, no shot-placement bravado, no "trophy of a lifetime" hype stacked on every page. One measured statement of the experience is enough.
- Measurements (horn length) only when verified and attributed (e.g. "measured by [scorer], [year]").

### Conservation claims

Only claim what can be backed by a document or a named programme. Acceptable now, from the brochures: hunts are in community-controlled areas; permits are allocated through an open government auction; the company employs local guides. Do not state percentages, population figures or "sustainable" outcomes without a source. When a source exists, cite it.

### Legal and permit language

- Always write the licence as **Govt. Licence No. 2087** (short form) or **Government Licence No. 2087** (in sentences). Retire "Govt. Licence No: ID 2087" (cover variant) — one format everywhere.
- Say **"trophy hunting permit"** (the brochure term) and **"provincial shooting licence"**.
- State the auction mechanism verbatim in pricing contexts: "Permits are allocated to Pakistani outfitters through an open government auction. Minor increases are absorbed by Hunt Venture Safaris; a substantial rise in permit fees will be communicated and invoiced separately."
- Never imply a guaranteed trophy or guaranteed success. Never say "no paperwork needed".
- "Legal hunting period" and "Recommended period" are distinct; keep both labels.

### Numbers, units and dates

- **Metres first.** "2,500–4,700 m". Where the brochure gives feet (Dureji), give metres first and feet in brackets: "approx. 150–915 m (500–3,000 ft), flat terrain".
- Thousands separator: comma. "4,700", not "4700" or "4.700" (except in Spanish copy, which uses a point or thin space).
- **Dates:** day month, no ordinals: "15 October – 30 April". Months in full in prose; three-letter abbreviations only in tight tables ("15 Oct – 30 Apr").
- **Seasons:** "2027–28" in data, "Season 2027–2028" on covers.
- **Durations:** "20 days · 15 hunting days".
- **Start and end:** "Islamabad to Islamabad" (prose) or "Islamabad–Islamabad" (tables). Karachi to Karachi for the Dureji combo.
- Phone numbers in international format with spaces: "+92 311 166 7829".

### Spelling and style

- **British English** throughout: licence (noun), organise, specialise, personalised, metres, colour, programme, enquiry.
- Note: "license" as a verb stays British-correct only as "licensed" — "Government-licensed outfitter".
- Use **Himalaya** (not "Himalayas") for the range, as in the Profile brochure; fix the cover lines that say "Himalayas".
- **Gilgit-Baltistan** with a hyphen, no spaces (fix "GILGIT - BALTISTAN" on covers).
- Do not use exclamation marks.

## 6. Photography and imagery

### Art direction

1. **Scale first.** The mountain is the hero. Prefer wide frames where the person and animal are small against the range.
2. **The hunter with the animal, with respect.** Animal posed naturally, head up, legs tucked, tongue in, eyes closed or clean; hunter beside or behind, not standing over it. Clean the animal before photographing.
3. **No blood visible.** Not on the animal, the snow, the hands or the clothing. Crop or reject.
4. **Rifles safe.** Rifle resting on the ground or across the pack, muzzle away from people and camera. Never pointed at the camera, never brandished, never held aloft.
5. **Natural light.** Golden hour and high-altitude daylight; no on-camera flash at night (it flattens and makes animals look grim).
6. **Include the people who make it happen.** Shikar guides, cooks, porters, community members. They are proof of the community pillar.
7. **Honest colour.** No heavy filters or HDR. Mild grading towards the field palette is fine.
8. **Consent.** Identifiable clients appear only with written permission.

## 7. Iconography and graphic devices

**Keep from the brochures:**

- **Brass hairline rule.** A short brass rule under section headings, and full-width rules separating photography from text. One weight only (hairline). Never a thick bar, never a left-border on cards.
- **Eyebrow labels.** Small, tracked, uppercase brass labels above headings and data ("AREA", "HUNTING PERIOD").
- **Itinerary timeline.** Vertical brass line with dot markers; the hunting-days marker is the single crimson dot. This is a pattern for the design system to build.

**Icons.** Thin-line, single-weight, monochrome (white or graphite; brass only for small inline markers). Subjects limited to the practical: calendar, altitude, duration, location, permit/document, phone, WhatsApp, email. No cartoon animals, no crosshairs or reticles, no bullet or rifle icons.

**Signature device — the altitude line.** One device, used consistently: a single brass hairline tracing a ridge profile, with the hunt's altitude range set against it in small figures ("2,500 m" at the low point, "4,700 m" at the high). It turns the brochures' most distinctive fact — these hunts happen at altitude — into a visual. Use it once per page: under the hero, as the divider into a hunt's "at a glance" block, or across the base of the trade-fair banner. Do not combine it with other ornaments, and do not fill it or animate it beyond a single slow draw (respect reduced motion). The design system specifies its stroke and construction.

## 9. Multilingual

**English is the primary language** of the brand, the website and all contracts.

**Spanish** serves the Cinegética audience. Translate in this order:

1. The trade-fair banner and a one-page hunt sheet per species (season, area, dates, altitude, duration, inclusions).
2. A Spanish landing page on the website covering the five hunts, the licence and the contact route (linked from the stand QR code).
3. WhatsApp greeting, away and quick-reply messages.
4. Inclusions/exclusions and the permit-auction pricing note — these must be translated by a professional because they function as terms.
5. Full species brochures, after the above are approved.

Rules: use a native Spanish speaker with hunting vocabulary (e.g. "caza", "trofeo", "permiso de caza", "guía"); keep species names in Spanish with the English trade name and scientific name alongside on first mention; keep "Hunt Venture Safaris" and "Govt. Licence No. 2087" untranslated; use Spanish number formats (4.700 m) and date order (15 de octubre – 30 de abril). Do not machine-translate terms and conditions.

## 10. Open questions for the client

1. **Vector logo:** does an original vector (AI/EPS/SVG/CDR) exist anywhere, e.g. with the first designer or printer? If not, approve commissioning a redraw.
2. **Wordmark typeface:** which font is the "HUNT VENTURE SAFARIS (PVT) LIMITED" set in? Do you hold a licence for Argent CF (brochure cover) for web use?
3. **Bone plate colour:** is the cover plate a deliberate brand colour or a printer's default? Confirm we can standardise it.
4. **Licence format:** confirm the official wording — "Govt. Licence No. 2087" or "ID 2087" — and whether a copy can be shown on the website.
5. **"Pioneering" and "most trusted":** what supports these claims (first licensed outfitter in Gilgit-Baltistan? number of hunts guided? client references we may quote)?
6. **Conservation and community:** what share of each permit fee goes to the community, and is there a document or named programme we may cite?
7. **Photography rights:** do we have permission from the clients shown in each photograph, and who took them? Who owns the copyright?
8. **Photos 03, 18, 19:** confirm species, location and year.
9. **Heritage photographs:** can you supply years, places and names for photos 00, 08, 13 (and others in your archive)?
10. **WhatsApp:** which number is on WhatsApp Business, and who answers it? Which languages?
11. **Email domain:** can we set up an address on huntventuresafaris.com?
12. **Scientific names:** confirm the names for Blanford Urial, Sindh Ibex and Punjab Urial, and whether to keep *Capra ibex sibirica* for Himalayan Ibex.
13. **Punjab Urial:** will there be a full detail sheet (dates, altitude, start/end city)?
14. **Office address:** please confirm the spelling "Hotel Riveria" (vs "Riviera").
15. **Spanish:** who will review Spanish translations? Is there a Spanish-speaking agent partner?
16. **Measured trophies:** do you have verified horn measurements (SCI or CIC scores) we may publish?
